The French Riviera isn't only beaches and superyachts. Barely an hour inland from Nice, the **Roya Valley** carves down from the Mercantour peaks toward the sea in a series of deep gorges, turquoise pools and rushing rapids. This is the coast's whitewater playground — and its capital is the little town of **Breil-sur-Roya**. If you want adrenaline, cold clear water and scenery most visitors never see, canyoning and rafting here deliver.

## What Is Canyoning?

**Canyoning** (canyoneering in the US) means descending a mountain gorge by following the watercourse itself. Instead of walking around obstacles, you go through them: **swimming** across pools, **sliding** down natural water chutes, **jumping** from ledges into deep basins, and **abseiling** (rappelling) down waterfalls on a rope. A qualified guide leads the route, sets up the ropes and briefs you at every drop.

It's part hiking, part climbing, part swimming — a full-body adventure in spectacular, otherwise-inaccessible scenery. Routes range from gentle, playful descents suitable for families and first-timers to vertical, technical canyons for thrill-seekers. In the Roya, most descents take a half to a full day, including the short approach walk in and the walk out.

## What Is Whitewater Rafting?

**Whitewater rafting** is descending a river's rapids aboard an inflatable raft, paddling as a team under a guide's commands. On the Roya you sit in a sturdy inflatable, life jacket and helmet on, and work together to steer through the "white water" — the churning, foamy sections where the river drops over rocks and ledges.

The Roya offers a genuinely exciting run of rapids in a narrow, wild gorge, yet local centres also run calmer stretches perfect for beginners and children. Variations you'll see advertised include **canoe-raft** and **kayak-raft** (smaller craft you paddle solo or in pairs, giving more control), **hydrospeed** (riding the rapids on a float board with fins) and **hot-dog** inflatable canoes. All the gear — helmet, life jacket, wetsuit and paddle — is provided; you just bring a swimsuit and towel.

## Where It Happens: The Roya Valley & Its Canyons

The **Roya river** rises near the Col de Tende on the French–Italian border and flows south through the Mercantour National Park past Tende, Saorge, Breil-sur-Roya and La Brigue before crossing into Italy. **Breil-sur-Roya** (postcode 06540), a scenic village on the river with its own little lake, is the hub for both sports — most guides and shops are based here.

The area's star attractions:

- **The Maglia canyon** — 15 minutes from Breil, the jewel of Alpes-Maritimes canyoning and rated among the top canyons in Europe. Turquoise pools, big slides, jumps and abseils. Water is a bracing 13–15°C, so the wetsuit matters.
- **Audin (Fanghetto)** — an easy, family-friendly discovery canyon just over the Italian border, 10 minutes away.
- **Barbaira** — near Rocchetta Nervina in Italy, a hidden gem of crystal-clear corridors and natural pools.
- **Carléva and Morghé** — quieter and more athletic routes, Morghé featuring the valley's biggest abseils.
- **The Roya rapids around Saorge and Breil** — the whitewater for rafting; calmer water lies further south.

## Season & Opening Times

Both activities are **weather- and water-dependent and run on a reservation basis** — you book a departure time with a centre rather than turning up.

- **Rafting season:** late spring to early autumn (roughly **April/May to September**). The biggest, most exciting flows come after the **May–June snowmelt**.
- **Canyoning season:** generally **June to October**, when the water warms and levels stabilise. Breil's mild climate can stretch this from April to October in a good year.
- **Peak availability:** **July and August**, with the widest choice of daily departures.
- **Typical daily departures:** morning (around 9:00) and early afternoon (around 14:00). Half-day trips run about 2–3 hours; full canyon days (like the Maglia) around 5 hours including approach.

Because exact timetables change with conditions, always **book ahead and confirm your slot** — especially in high summer. A few of the established local operators:

- **Nice Rafting / Mat & Eau** — rafting, canoe-raft, hydrospeed and canyoning, based in Breil-sur-Roya (with a changing-room shop in the village).
- **Roya Évasion** — canyoning (including the Maglia), canoe-rafting and mountain biking, Breil-sur-Roya.
- **Canyon & Merveilles** — canyoning and via ferrata specialists across the Roya and Maglia.

Indicative prices: around **€35–40** for a 2-hour rafting trip and from about **€62** for a half-day canyoning outing.

## How to Get to the Roya Valley

### By car (most flexible)

From Nice, head north on the **D2204** through l'Escarène and over the Col de Braus/Brouis into the Roya gorges — about **1 to 1.5 hours** to Breil-sur-Roya. From the coast you can also approach via the **A8 motorway** then inland. The drive itself is spectacular; there's parking in Breil near the centres. Coming from Menton or Monaco, you can loop up via Sospel.

### By train (scenic & car-free)

This is one of the great rail journeys in France. The **SNCF Nice–Tende–Cuneo line**, known as the **"Train des Merveilles,"** climbs from **Nice-Ville** up the Roya gorges to **Breil-sur-Roya** in about **1.5 hours**, with several departures a day. Breil's station is a short walk from the village and the activity centres, so you genuinely don't need a car. Trains continue on to Saorge, Fontan, Tende and into Italy.

### By bus

Regional **Zou!** buses serve the Roya valley from the coast, but services are limited and slower than the train. For most visitors the **train is the better car-free option**; if you do come by bus, plan connections carefully and allow extra time.

## Tips for a Great Day on the Water

- **Book ahead** and reconfirm your departure the day before — conditions can shift after rain.
- **Wear old trainers** you don't mind soaking; the technical kit is provided but footwear usually isn't.
- **Come reasonably fit and water-confident** — both sports involve swimming, and canyoning adds jumps and abseils.
- **Bring a snack and dry clothes** for after; the water is cold even in summer.
- **Combine it** with a slow ride on the Train des Merveilles or a wander round Saorge for a perfect Riviera-hinterland day.

The Roya Valley is proof that the Côte d'Azur's best adventures aren't always by the sea. Cold turquoise water, wild gorges and a train that climbs into the mountains — it's the French Riviera at its most exhilarating, and it's closer than you think.
