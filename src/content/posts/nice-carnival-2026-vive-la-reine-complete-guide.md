*Nice Carnival 2026 complete guide*

Get ready for one of Europe's most spectacular carnival celebrations! From February 11 to March 1, 2026, Nice transforms into a dazzling festival of colors, music, and unforgettable moments. This year's theme, "Long Live the Queen," promises a historic celebration honoring the great female heroines of history and fiction. Whether you're a first-time visitor or a seasoned carnival enthusiast, this comprehensive guide will help you plan the perfect carnival experience.

### The Magic of Nice Carnival 2026

The Nice Carnival stands as the premier international carnival in France and one of the most important winter events on the Côte d'Azur. For nearly two weeks, the entire city pulses with the energy of elaborate parades, magnificent floats, and countless celebrations. With over 400,000 visitors expected, this isn't just a local festival—it's a global spectacle.

The 2026 edition marks a historic turning point. After decades of masculine dominance, the carnival is celebrating femininity and empowerment with the theme "Vive la Reine!" (Long Live the Queen!). The seventeen mechanized floats, standing between 8 and 20 meters high, will tell stories of extraordinary female figures, creating a powerful visual spectacle intertwined with contemporary pop culture references.

### When to Experience the Magic: Dates and Duration

**Carnival Dates: February 11 – March 1, 2026.** This fifteen-day celebration is the longest and one of the most comprehensive carnivals in Europe. From the opening until the closing celebration with the burning of the King Carnival and fireworks display, the city remains in constant festive mode.

### The Carnival Route: Where the Magic Happens

The main carnival parades ("Corsos") follow a traditional route: **Place Masséna** (the heart of the carnival, with the grand opening ceremony and the most elaborate stands); the **Promenade des Anglais** (between Avenue de Verdun and the seafront); the **Promenade du Paillon** (location of the Carnival Village, with free activities, food vendors, and cultural performances); and **Vieux-Nice** (the medieval old town, host to smaller parades and events like the Waiter's Race).

Viewing stands (tribunes) are positioned at key points, particularly along Place Masséna. The pedestrian zones at the foot of certain stands offer free or low-cost alternatives.

### Complete Program 2026: Day-by-Day Highlights

**Opening Week (February 11-15)**

- **Wed Feb 11, 4:00 PM — Le Grand Charivari (FREE):** the opening ceremony at the Maison de Carnaval with deambulations, concerts, and happenings.
- **Fri Feb 13, 7:00 PM — Le Bal du Veglione:** the Opera House transforms into a ballroom, beginning with the Nice Philharmonic Orchestra.
- **Sat Feb 14, 1:00 PM — La Carnavalina (FREE):** troupes and artists embodying the DNA of Nice Carnival; welcomes the foreign honored guests.
- **Sat Feb 14, 9:00 PM — Corso Carnavalesque Illuminé (Opening Illuminated Parade):** the first nighttime parade, floats up to 20 metres high glowing with thousands of lights.
- **Sun Feb 15, 9:00 AM — Course des Garçons de Café (Waiter's Race), Vieux-Nice:** participants dressed as waiters balance platters through narrow streets.
- **Sun Feb 15, 12:00 PM — Carnaval des Frenchy, Theatre of Verdure:** a humorous, irreverent take on carnival traditions.

**Main Celebration Week (February 17-25)**

- **Tue Feb 17, 8:30 PM — Corso Carnavalesque Illuminé:** another spectacular evening parade.
- **Sun Feb 22, 2:30 PM — Corso Carnavalesque de Jour (Daytime Parade):** an ideal family experience in daylight.
- **Tue Feb 24, 8:30 PM — Corso Carnavalesque Illuminé:** the penultimate nighttime spectacle.
- **Wed Feb 25, 2:30 PM — Bataille des Fleurs (Flower Battle):** twenty floats decorated with thousands of fresh flowers; over 100,000 flowers distributed.

**Closing Week (February 26-March 1)**

- **Thu Feb 26, 6:00–10:00 PM — Carnival Village Activities, Promenade du Paillon:** extended evening hours.
- **Fri Feb 27, 8:30 PM — Lou QueernaVal (LGBTQ+ Carnival):** France's first gay carnival, celebrating inclusion.
- **Sat Feb 28, 8:30 PM — Final Corso Carnavalesque Illuminé & Burning of King Carnival:** the last parade, the ceremonial burning of the King Carnival effigy at Quai des États-Unis, followed by fireworks.
- **Sun Mar 1, Morning — Le Grand Bain Collectif, Opera Beach:** hundreds plunge into the chilly Mediterranean.

### Getting There and Getting Around

**By Air** — Nice-Côte d'Azur Airport (NCE), one of Europe's major hubs. Flight times from Paris ~1.5 hours, prices from €60 return if booked in advance.

**By Train** — Nice-Ville railway station is only 15 minutes on foot from Place Masséna. During carnival, special ZOU! trains operate every 15 minutes between Cannes and Menton, with additional trains after evening parades.

**Local Transportation** — The Lignes d'Azur tram and bus network serves all carnival locations, with special carnival shuttle services during the festival.

### What to Expect

**The Floats:** Seventeen mechanized floats 8–20 metres tall, led by the King, Queen, and their son Carnavalon, depicting female heroes from history and fiction. **The Performers:** over 1,000 musicians and dancers from around the world. **The Flowers:** the Flower Battles, where performers throw thousands of mimosas, gerberas and lilies. **The Atmosphere:** festive, colorful, family-friendly yet with a sophisticated edge.

### Essential Tips for a Perfect Visit

**Planning:** book early · get a full head-to-toe costume for free pedestrian access · download the Lignes d'Azur app · arrive 1-2 hours early for good spots · comfortable shoes · pack layers · a waterproof bag for the flower battles · sunscreen.

**Safety & Logistics:** expect bag checks; avoid prohibited items; stay hydrated; keep copies of documents; save emergency contacts.

**Cultural & Food:** taste socca, pissaladière, ganses and pan bagnat; visit the Cours Saleya market; explore Vieux-Nice; be respectful of local traditions; have a costume ready.
