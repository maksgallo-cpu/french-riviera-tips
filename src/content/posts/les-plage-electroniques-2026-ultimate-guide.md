Every August, the golden sand in front of the Palais des Festivals in Cannes stops being a postcard backdrop and turns into one of the wildest dancefloors in Europe. Les Plages Électroniques, France's biggest beach party, transforms the Croisette into an open-air arena where sunset cocktails give way to fifteen hours of non-stop electronic music, feet in the sand, eyes on the Mediterranean horizon. Whether you are a first-timer wondering what all the fuss is about or a returning festival-goer planning your twentieth summer on that beach, this guide covers everything you need: the story behind the festival, the 2026 lineup, ticket prices, how to actually get to Cannes, and a carefully curated list of hotels for every budget.

## A Beach Party Turns Twenty

Les Plages Électroniques was born in 2006 with a deceptively simple idea: bring the biggest names in electronic music to the sand of Cannes and let the sea do the rest. That first edition drew around 7,000 curious dancers. Two decades later, the concept has become an institution of the European summer calendar, pulling in roughly 60,000 festival-goers over three days, spread across multiple stages built directly on the beach and terrace of the Palais des Festivals et des Congrès.

2026 marks the 20th anniversary of the festival, and organisers have gone all in to celebrate it. The site has grown by more than 6,750 square metres, now stretching to include the Esplanade Pantiero and the Gare Maritime, for a total footprint of roughly 40,000 square metres. Expect brand-new stages, freshly designed immersive spaces, a giant food court, a skatepark takeover, a pétanque tournament, and after-parties that spill into downtown Cannes once the main stages go quiet.

Beyond the music, the festival has built a reputation as a full lifestyle weekend. Think pop-up restaurants facing the water, beauty corners between sets, brand activations along the boardwalk, and the Rotonde du Palais reborn as the Unexplored Club, an indoor dancefloor with a 360-degree stage. It is equal parts music festival, beach club, and street party.

## Dates and 2026 Lineup

Les Plages Électroniques 2026 takes place from Friday 7 to Sunday 9 August, with doors opening at 2 PM and music running until roughly 12:30 AM on the main stages (after-parties push things further into the night at partner venues around town). For its anniversary edition, the festival has pulled together one of its strongest bills in years.

- **Friday 7 August:** Martin Garrix headlines, alongside Mosimann, Nico Moreno, Candice, Djudah and a long list of supporting acts; the after-party belongs to Mind Against.
- **Saturday 8 August:** Amélie Lens, Marshmello, PLK and Vladimir Cauchemar take over the main stage, with Mathame closing out the night.
- **Sunday 9 August:** DJ Snake curates the entire day under his own banner, Pardon My French, bringing along Anetha, Patrick Mason, Salvatore Ganacci and Dany Neville, with Maceo Plex on after-party duty.

Roughly forty additional artists complete the bill across the festival's secondary stages, covering everything from techno and house to disco and French rap.

## Ticket Prices and Where to Buy Them

Tickets are sold in limited quantities and prices increase as each release tier sells out, so booking early genuinely pays off.

- **1-Day Pass:** from roughly €57 to €195, depending on the day and how early you book.
- **3-Day Pass:** from roughly €135 to €515, depending on the release tier and any after-party add-ons.

Please note that the festival runs on a fully cashless payment system, so budget a little extra for a rechargeable wristband or card once you are on site — cash will not be accepted for food, drinks or merchandise.

Official tickets are sold exclusively through the festival's own platform and its authorised partner. Official website: [plages-electroniques.com/billetterie](http://plages-electroniques.com/billetterie). Authorised reseller: [See Tickets](https://www.seetickets.com/). Always buy through these official channels — third-party resale sites and social media listings for sold-out dates are a common source of counterfeit tickets.

## How to Get to Cannes

**By Car** — Cannes is served directly by the A8 motorway ("La Provençale"). Take the Cannes exit and follow signs toward the Palais des Festivals. During the festival, expect heavy traffic on the Croisette; arriving early or using a park-and-ride option outside the centre is a smart move.

**By Train** — Cannes has its own SNCF railway station, Gare de Cannes, in the town centre and only about a ten-minute walk from the Croisette and the festival site. Regular TER regional trains and direct TGV services from Paris (roughly five hours) run during summer. For a car-free festival weekend, the train is by far the most stress-free option.

**By Plane** — The nearest airport is Nice Côte d'Azur Airport (NCE), roughly 30 minutes from Cannes by car or shuttle. From the airport you can pick up a rental car, hop in a taxi, or take one of the regular airport shuttle buses and express coach lines. Book airport transfers in advance during festival weekends.

**By Boat** — Cannes' Vieux Port and Port Canto sit just steps from the festival site. Trans Côte d'Azur runs scheduled seasonal maritime shuttles connecting Cannes with Nice, Monaco, the Corniche d'Or and Saint-Tropez (April to October). In July and August, a dedicated Estérel shuttle also links Cannes with Mandelieu-La Napoule and Théoule-sur-Mer.

**By Bus** — Cannes is served by the local Palm Bus network. For longer distances, FlixBus and BlaBlaBus connect Cannes to major French and European cities.

## Practical Tips for Festival Weekend

- Book accommodation as early as possible — availability drops sharply once the lineup is announced.
- Bring a valid photo ID; the festival is strictly 18 and over.
- Pack light and leave large bags at home — most stages enforce clear-bag or no-bag policies.
- Load your cashless wristband or payment card in advance; the site does not accept cash.
- Wear comfortable shoes suited to sand, and bring a light layer for after midnight.
- If using the Estérel boat shuttle or a Trans Côte d'Azur crossing, check the seasonal timetable in advance.

## Final Thoughts

Twenty years in, Les Plages Électroniques has lost none of its magic. There is something genuinely special about dancing to a world-class DJ set with your feet literally in the Mediterranean, the Palais des Festivals lit up behind you and the Lérins Islands fading into the dark ahead. Book your tickets early, sort your travel and hotel well ahead of August, and show up ready for three days that define what a French Riviera summer is supposed to feel like.
